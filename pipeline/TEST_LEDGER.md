# Test ledger — every model test, kept or not

Why this exists: by mid-October we had run ~45 tests. At a 5% false-positive rate, two or three "wins" are flukes. Rules:
1. Every test gets a row here, including the ones that failed.
2. **Holdout:** 2026 Week 7 onward is untouched evaluation data for everything added in October. Do not tune anything on it.
3. **Season-end re-check (after Week 18):** re-score each KEPT change on Weeks 7–18 only (walk-forward, same metric as when it was adopted). Remove any change that is worse than the version without it. Re-check REJECTED changes that were close (marked "revisit").
4. A change ships only if it beats the current model on seasons it was not fit on, or is a documented judgment call (marked JUDGMENT, with the trade-off).

## Nov 2: awards team assignment fix (season_model.py)
Bug: player-season totals took `"team": "last"`, i.e. the alphabetically last team a player had any stat with. Defensive tallies are keyed by defteam, so punt-coverage tackles put Arvell Reese (NYG) on TEN, Kendal Daniels (ATL) on NO and Elijah Ponder (NE) on SEA; traded players could also land on their old team. The team also feeds the award models (team wins), so it changed the numbers: DROY Reese 16.5% -> 24.8% (now with NYG's record), Bailey 66.9% -> 60.3%. Fix: team with most of the player's production that season; current season overridden by his latest team in the weekly roster file. Season tab unchanged.

## Nov 2: scorecard spread sign fix + accuracy tables
Bug: the site's game `spread` is the home team's expected margin (nflverse spread_line, + = home favored), but scorecard.py graded "home covers" as mg + spread > 0 and the closing line's margin miss as |mg + spread|. Effect: home covers showed 67% (real 48%) and the line's average miss 15.0 (real 10.2), which made the model look ahead of the market on margins when it is behind (11.0 vs 10.2 over 27 games). Fixed to mg - spread. Paper-bet grading (paper_lines.py) already converted the sign and was right.
New (Clinton): Record tab opens with "How accurate is the model?" - scorecard.json `accuracy` (season + each week). Fixed verdict rules: player stats need 100+; "Runs low/high" when |lean| >= 5% of the projection and t >= 2; "Ranges too narrow/wide" when inside the 10th-90th range is off 80% by more than max(4 pts, 2 SE). Yes/no calls need 30 games (winner, totals, covers), 100 (TD) or 40 (INT); "Happens more/less often than it says" at |z| >= 2 overall, else "Too cautious/confident on its likeliest calls" at |z| >= 2 in the top fifth of chances.

## Nov 2: range grading for count stats (scorecard.py)
Diagnosis (Clinton asked why rushing ranges looked so narrow): 135 of 257 graded backs were projected for 3 or fewer carries; their floor is 0 and 61% got 0. The half-credit tie rule counted each as half below the floor, so rushing showed 40% outside the range. Counting ties as inside overcorrects the other way (receptions/carries then read "too wide"). Now: randomized PIT from the 21 published quantiles (a result's probability band is split across below-floor / inside / above-ceiling). Check: every stat's middle half now lands at 45-56% (target 50%). Season read: receptions, targets about right; rushing yards 79% inside but 15% above the ceiling; receiving yards 75% inside, 15% above; PPR 14% above; carries run low. New verdicts "Ceilings too low/high" when above-ceiling is off 10% by more than max(3 pts, 2 SE). Common thread: big games are under-rated (ceilings), not busts.

## Nov 2: ceilings (tests/t_ceiling_sim.py) - NO CHANGE to the simulator; the cause is upstream
Live Weeks 7-8: 14-15% of rushing yards, receiving yards and PPR beat the ceiling (target 10%). Walk-forward simulator check, 660 games (2023-24 fit, 2025-26 held out), current settings: above-ceiling rec yds 10.7/10.8%, rush yds 9.6/10.5%, PPR 11.5/11.7%; floors 9-11%; middle half 47-52%. The simulator's spread is right on past seasons. More dispersion (GAME_EFF_SD 0.46, shapes x0.55) only moves PPR above-ceiling to ~10.6% and pushes rushing's middle half to 54%: not adopted.
Where the live excess comes from: the published means ran low. Rescaling each result by the stat's live mean bias takes above-ceiling to ~12% (within noise). Matching the published Sunday snapshots to the walk-forward raw model for the same 181 players (Weeks 7-8, p_play 0.9+, played): published receiving yards = 0.913 x raw, actual = 0.991 x raw (raw model on target, published 8.5% low); carries published = 0.935 x raw. The cut is largest for low projections (0-15 yds: x0.76; 15-35: x0.85; 60+: x1.00). Suspect: proj_project.py's team scaling to the QB's line (scale 0.87-0.98 every team, every week) dividing the passing game over a live pool that includes players who won't touch the ball; the walk-forward pool only contains players who played. NEXT TEST: refit the team-scaling layer on the live pool composition (or weight each player's share by his chance of a real role), judged on archived pre-kickoff snapshots.

## Nov 2 (later): team-scaling "dilution" test (tests/t_pool_dilution.py) - NO dilution; the gap was a grading selection effect
Counting every published pool player in Weeks 7-8 (27 games, 549 non-QB rows; a player with no stat line scores 0): projected team receiving yards 12,060 vs 12,092 caught by pool players (12,381 by all players, i.e. 2.5% to unlisted players vs POOL_FRAC's 3%). The team-scaling layer is right. The "published 8.5% below raw" comparison above was apples to oranges: the walk-forward raw model is fit on players who played, i.e. it is an if-he-plays number. Carries: projected 1,175 vs 1,281 by pool backs (Weeks 7-8 game script; 8-14 band 1.29x, as found earlier).
The real bias was in the scorecard: it graded only players who touched the ball, which selects on good days. 86 listed players were on the field with no target or carry (e.g. Omar Cooper Jr. 50 snaps, Cade Otton 57) and were dropped from the averages and miscounted as no-shows. Fix (scorecard.py): grade every listed player with offensive snaps > 0 (a 0-target day is a 0); no-show = no offensive snap. Season read after the fix: receptions 2.3 proj / 2.1 actual, receiving yards 23.6 / 23.7, PPR 7.9 / 7.8 (no lean); rushing yards +1.1, carries +0.5; no-shows 2 of 325 (0.6%). Ranges: receiving yards 74.5% inside (13% below, 12% above), PPR 75% (13.5% / 12%), rushing 79% (8% / 14%) - slightly narrow on both sides for yards and PPR over two weeks, while the simulator is calibrated on 2023-26 (t_ceiling_sim). Verdict: no model change; watch until 150+ more results per stat.

## Nov 2: questionable players (Clinton: are they double-discounted?) - no bug, NO CHANGE
Live Weeks 7-8 (tests/t_failures.py): 22 players with a chance-to-play below 1 who did play beat their PPR projection by +4.0 (median +2.2); Allgeier alone (6.7 -> 36.1) is a third of it; without him +2.8 (about 1.9 SE).
Code check: no double discount. The simulator takes a questionable player's numbers only from runs where he dresses (proj_project.py), teammates' from all runs; td_project.py multiplies TD rates by p_play for the team total and divides it back out (lam / p_play) for the shown number; back_mult applies only to players returning from missed games.
History (walk-forward 2019-2026, RB/WR/TE who played; catches + yards points): questionable players produced 0.939 of projection vs 0.987 for unlisted players (1,564 cases; 2019-23 0.931, 2024+ 0.959). So if anything the "if he plays" number for questionable players should be ~5% LOWER, the opposite of the live read. The live gap is noise. A 0.95 discount is a candidate (about 2.5 SE) but was not adopted: the Oct inj_test usage check (0.97) was not significant and the live weeks point the other way.
Also from the same breakdown: returning players eased in (back_mult) came in -0.9 vs projection (13 players; mean 5.0 -> 4.1), news lead backs +2.8 (7 players). Both too small to act on.
